Getting help

Email williamscott701@gmail.com. One person reads it, and replies are usually within a couple of days. Telling me your iPhone model and iOS version saves a round trip.

Daysheet is a record-keeping tool, not a compliance guarantee. It works out staff-to-child ratios and group sizes from published rules and from the dates of birth you enter. Your licensing authority is the authority — state rules are amended, and your own licence may carry conditions stricter than the published rule. Where they differ, your licence wins, and you should enter its numbers under Your own limits. I cannot tell you what your licence requires, and please do not email me to ask: a wrong answer from a stranger could cost you your registration.

Common questions

Who is this for?

The one-person operation. A nanny with three children, a childminder with six, a small home daycare. The existing software — Procare, brightwheel, Lillio — is centre-management for facilities with fifty children and is priced per seat. Nobody was serving the person who just needs to send four good sheets before going home, which is what Daysheet does.

Do parents need an app?

No, and that is the point. There is no parent app, no invitation to accept, no account for anyone to make. Daysheet turns the day into a PDF and hands it to the iOS share sheet — you send it however you already talk to that family, whether that is a message, an email or a printout at the door.

Where do the ratios come from?

Each state's own published licensing rule for child care centres, read directly and cited in the app so you can look it up. Tap Ratio rules to see the agency and the regulation behind whatever table you have selected. The default is the national guidance in Caring for Our Children, 4th edition, which is stricter than almost every state minimum — so an operator who has not yet chosen is never shown a number laxer than their licence allows.

Why isn't my state in the list?

Because I could not read its published table well enough to ship it. Virginia's group-size table could not be retrieved in full for three- and four-year-olds, and Wisconsin's rule was unreachable. Guessing would have been worse than leaving them out. Use Your own limits and type in what your licence says — that is the right answer for anyone whose licence differs from the published rule, in any state.

The ratio table for my state doesn't match my licence.

Then your licence is right and the table is not the rule for you. Home-based settings are licensed under a different part of the code than centres in every state, quality-rated licences are often stricter, and individual conditions are common. Enter your own numbers.

Does Daysheet apply nap-time ratio relaxations?

No, deliberately. Many states relax ratios while children are sleeping, under conditions that vary enormously — how many are actually asleep, whether an adult is in the room, whether there are infants in the group, how long it may last. Daysheet reports the ratio for the children who are present. Applying a relaxation the app cannot verify would be the wrong kind of confident.

How does a child move up a ratio band?

By having a birthday. The band is worked out from the date of birth every time, so a child who turns two overnight is counted under the two-year-old ratio the next morning with no action from you. A child born on 29 February moves up on 1 March — the conservative reading, which keeps them in the more staff-intensive band a day longer.

What does the sleep figure mean?

The nap total for the day, shown against what is typical for that age. The age norms come from the National Sleep Foundation's published recommendations (Hirshkowitz et al., Sleep Health, 2015), which are figures per twenty-four hours and therefore include the night. A childcare setting sees the daytime only, so the app says that under every nap total. It is context for a parent, never an assessment of a child.

What makes an incident record "complete"?

Daysheet checks each record against the field list in Caring for Our Children, 4th edition, Standard 9.4.1.9 — the child, the time, the exact place, what they were doing, what happened, the part of the body, any equipment involved, what you did, who you told and when, and who wrote it. It tells you what is missing while you can still remember the answer, because the moment a record matters is the only moment you cannot fix it. Your licensing authority decides what you must actually keep; this is a completeness check against a published standard, not a legal test.

Can Daysheet tell me whether to give a medicine?

No. It records that you gave one — the medication, the dose, the time, who gave it, and which written parental authorisation it was covered by. If what you type matches an allergy or medical flag on that child's record, it stops and shows you the flag. That is a safety net, not advice.

What happens if a child has an allergy?

Flags appear at the top of the meal screen for the right child, loudly. A flag marked anaphylaxis will not let you save a meal until you confirm you have read it. That friction is deliberate and it is the only place in the app where friction is the right answer.

Where do photos go?

Into Daysheet's own storage on your iPhone, and nowhere else. The app makes no network requests at all, so there is nowhere for them to go. The only way a photograph leaves the device is on a sheet you share yourself.

What is free, and what is Pro?

Free tracks two children and shows today's sheet on screen, which is genuinely useful — it is what you read out at the door. Pro adds unlimited children, the sheet as a PDF you can send, week and month summaries, ratio and group-size checks, incident and medication records, the attendance and billing export, and photo attachments.

How do I cancel?

Subscriptions are managed by Apple, not by this app: open the Settings app, tap your name, then Subscriptions. Cancelling stops the next renewal and you keep Pro until the period you have paid for ends.

A child left and I need their records.

Archive rather than delete. Archiving takes them off the register and keeps every sheet, attendance record, incident and medication log — which is exactly what gets asked for after a child has left. Deleting is permanent and is offered separately for that reason.

The widget is not updating.

Remove it and add it again. If it still shows nothing, open the app once — the widget reads a file the app writes, and there is nothing to read until the app has run after installing. The ratio widget is blank without Pro by design: the figure is never written to the file a free build can read.

Privacy in one line

Daysheet collects nothing, sends nothing and has no account. The full policy says so at length, and it matters more here than in most apps — this is somebody else's children's data.
